## Public observations: useful constraints, limited identification

The [NBA glossary](https://www.nba.com/stats/help/glossary) distinguishes tracking's **Contested Shot** distance definition from hustle's **Contested Shots** hand-up/pre-release definition (CC10/CN01). Preserve metric names **and contexts**. Neither supplies a hand-to-ball trajectory, recognition latency or legal-control measurement. A nearby defender behind the release is not equivalent to a correctly timed hand in front of it.

A dated [NBA table, May13,2022](https://www.nba.com/news/open-shots-key-entering-game-6-of-bucks-celtics-and-warriors-grizzlies) reports BOS **39/100 open** and **44/102 wide-open** attempts in its stated first-five-game MIL-series sample beyond10feet (CN02). These recompute to39.0% and43.1%, respectively. Denominator: **actual attempted shots in each bin**, pooling eligible2/3point attempts—not all offensive threats, all closeouts or possessions. The published0–2/2–4/4–6/6+foot categories are observation labels, not approved physical-response discontinuities or a verified boundary-inclusion protocol.

Those counts are descriptive checks, **not causal contest penalties**. Shooter quality, shot type/location, clock, pass readiness, closeout direction and which shots were declined all vary. A defender can deter an attempt without entering defended-FGA statistics; conditioning only on attempted shots selects a different population. OpponentFG% cannot isolate S37/S40/S45 or quantify the recovery cost after a pump fake. Small cells, role difficulty and shooter mix can produce noisy/nonmonotonic pooled results. No current dashboard percentage is promoted into a player estimate; rounded per-game search rows were not reconstructed into exact denominators.

Legality has primary support in [Rule12-B](https://official.nba.com/rule-no-12-fouls-and-penalties/) and the official written [ordinary landing-contact](https://videorulebook.nba.com/archive/defensive-foul-defender-makes-lower-body-contact-with-shooter/) and [reckless landing-contact](https://videorulebook.nba.com/archive/flagrant-foul-penalty-1-defender-reckless-contest-into-landing-space-shooter-turns-ankle/) examples (CC12–13). The narrations distinguish a defensive foul from a Flagrant1 case. Their videos were not reviewed, their dates are unverified, and they supply no actor foul probability or injury model. Closer/more aggressive contests must not automatically count as better legal defense.

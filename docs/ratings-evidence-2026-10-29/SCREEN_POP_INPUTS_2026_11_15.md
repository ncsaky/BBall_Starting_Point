## Roll versus pop under declared contexts

- A supplied reference catch can examine shooting while leaving screen/route quality unresolved. Towns' stronger source shooting case and Holmgren's release-access limitation are meaningful distinctions under matched preparation, shot distance and recovery pressure; they do not predict full-action conversion rates.
- A pop route must actually create a passing window, secure delivery and reach a shot opportunity before recovery. Turner/Horford have specific historical route support. Reid/Lopez have shooting support without equivalent retained route evidence. Leaving the latter unknown is not a conclusion that they cannot pop.
- Rolling can exploit paint space and finishing; popping can exploit a defender committing inside. The handler, defender, spacing, route distance and available pass determine the tradeoff. Holmgren's fluid-roll evidence and Wembanyama's paint threat should remain visible without assigning unsupported finish or read grades. Jokić's broader short-touch/passing repertoire must not disappear into a three-point-only comparison.
- **All eight T11 fields remain unknown.** Separate called-roll and called-pop conditions are legitimate coaching scenarios; neither is an innate numerical preference. No observed roll/pop distribution or target result is supplied. The eight-player packet does not require re-researching Gobert/Zubac or producing a full-player ranking.

S01 reference execution and S08 preparation must not both absorb the same contested-shot failure. S09 extension is not a second accuracy bonus. S26 route timing is not raw speed; S28 follow-up selection is not pass-family recognition. Screen body mass is not screen placement, legal contact or functional strength. Correlated observations are tagged with shared-evidence groups rather than treated as independent measurements.

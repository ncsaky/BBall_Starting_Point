## Product Direction

The target is sustained championship opportunity, not simply a valuable roster or repeated first-round appearances. Peak is a league-relative distinction. A team can be personally at its age/ability peak and still not have a credible championship peak. Mixed-age cores are allowed. A rebuilding team does not have to sell every good player, and an exiting team should look for a retool before dismantling the core.

The user's Memphis example is a decision-pattern illustration, not a verified historical chronology relied on by the model.

## Implemented

### Windows

`team_planning.window_assessments` uses existing lineup/fit-driven contention strength, a bounded sample-weighted record adjustment, and a separate age/upside/health development scenario. Elite-foundation benchmarks use active rostered players only, excluding retired players and free agents.

The four explicit states are rebuilding, entering, peak and exiting. Existing legacy phase names are adapters for current trading/signing consumers, not additional competing classifications. The strategy cache version is advanced so stale source contexts are rebuilt.

Peak requires top-six modeled league rank, proximity to the strongest roster, sufficient absolute strength, and a normalized strength weight meaningfully above the equal-team baseline. It is not guaranteed that any particular number of teams qualifies; equal-strength teams are not arbitrarily assigned different states by team ID. The index is NOT calibrated championship odds. Forward strength is a bounded development scenario, not guaranteed potential realization.

Credible but plateaued teams can remain entering; they are not automatically called rebuilders because they lack projected growth. Aging alone cannot disqualify an actually elite team from peak. Qualifying decline prompts an exiting/retool direction rather than an automatic teardown.

### Intent And Core Memory

Plans are stored per team in the save, not regenerated by dashboard reads. They review on calendar months, roster/transaction changes, completed postseason evidence and serious injury changes. Ordinary short absences do not rebuild every team's strategic context.

Core selection uses current basketball contribution and credible upside, with continuity slack for existing members. There is no same-age rule or mandatory count. Departed/retired players leave the core. Most cores in the short pilot had one to five members; larger genuine groups are permitted.

A new plan gives its core a trial through the following June. A premium acquisition involving meaningful outgoing investment extends the trial to at least 365 days after the trade, covering existing core partners too. The acquisition date remains saved if old transaction logs move into history storage.

Core trial commitments affect inferred trade-block placement and normal AI trade acceptance. A sufficiently exceptional return can still be accepted. Serious core injuries and recorded trade pressure permit a reasoned review; they do not force acceptance of an otherwise bad trade. Human-controlled teams are not bound by the AI commitment rule. Core membership also provides bounded extension and retention preferences rather than immunity.

Completed postseason outcomes are retained with the core. Repeated early exits and a flat future scenario request a ceiling/retool review after the trial; more patient GMs require more evidence. A review or exiting window can identify one replaceable/declining core piece for normal trade-market exploration. The system does not list the whole core at once or forcibly execute a teardown.

### Recover Value Before Cutting

Free-agent admission records a dated, concrete roster-space target when an upgrade would require cuts. It can do this at a full camp roster or when a bid is not currently fundable, without submitting an illegal offer.

Before free-agency resolution, the AI can seek a second-round pick in return for a non-core player projected to be cut. The prospective buyer must want to retain the player. Both teams must actually accept under the shared evaluator, and normal salary/ownership/recent-signing restrictions still apply. A bounded seller opportunity credit prices the avoided roster-space loss without changing the buyer's valuation. The real asset return, excluding that credit, must meet a 70% floor, or 85% for higher-value role players. Core assets and high-end premium assets are excluded from this discounted route.

Search is bounded to 12 full trade evaluations per phase and limited buyer/pick options. It is an additional avenue, not a mandatory sale and not a fake buyer. A vanished target or clearly superior competing offer cancels speculative liquidation. After a sale, a new bid still needs current cap room and a viable roster plan; it does not guarantee the free agent signs.

An existing cap-dump path had overwritten buyer consent after checking only legality. That shortcut was removed. Shared automatic free-agency trade processing now rejects candidates not approved by both sides instead of manufacturing `accepted_by_all`.

## Explicit Limits

This is a first integrated planning model, not a solved multi-season title optimizer. It does not yet compare several fully simulated alternative cores, calculate calibrated championship probabilities, or infer whether every past playoff exit was bad luck versus a structural ceiling. Injury insurance, specialist combinations, cap succession and development opportunity valuation remain approximations within the existing role and fit system.

The new sale search uses current available free agents and concrete bids/targets. It does not assume a future star will become available and sell assets years ahead of time. Natural sale examples and additional return structures deserve focused follow-up, not forced transaction quotas.

Contracts remain a simple guaranteed/not-guaranteed negotiation choice. No partial-guarantee UI, buyout implementation, waiver-claim queue, stretch election or set-off implementation was added here.

## Buyouts And Waivers: Next Design

A buyout should require player consent to give up an explicit amount, influenced by expected playing time, another opportunity, age and remaining protected pay. The amount still owed remains a salary obligation; this must not become a delete-contract/cap-amnesty button. Use the existing separate obligation ledger and preserve a permanent record of the negotiated reduction.

A waiver claim is an acquisition of the existing contract, not a fresh minimum offer. The proposed workflow is a two-day pending claim period, standings-based priority, financial/roster eligibility checks at submission and resolution, and one atomic transfer of ownership and obligation. If nobody claims, the player becomes an ordinary free agent and the old team's protected obligation remains. Salary relief, eligible exceptions, re-acquisition restrictions and claimed-player trade restrictions need explicit tests before enabling this workflow.

Rule references: [NBA CBA 101](https://cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2024/11/2024-25-CBA-101.pdf), salary obligations and set-off; [NBA Constitution and By-Laws](https://cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/NBA-Consitution-By-Laws-June-2024.pdf), sections 5.03-5.06 for the 48-hour period, priority and acquired contract rights; [Basketball Reference glossary](https://www.basketball-reference.com/contracts/glossary.html), recorded buyout adjustments. Detailed implementation still needs the complete applicable CBA, not a claim that this short design covers every exception.

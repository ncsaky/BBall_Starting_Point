# Injuries and return to play vs Basketball Index

**The game runs out of new injuries before the stretch run.** Both seeds hit 565 injuries, about 19 per team, and a typical injury lasts 3 days versus 6 in Basketball Index. Sim players miss 4.5 games per 82 rostered team games.  
**Players come back faster and play closer to their usual minutes.** In Basketball Index, players who missed 6–10 games played 16% fewer minutes in their first game back. Most simulated returns carried zero rust into their first game.  
**Start with longer, body-specific absences and a coach-led return to minutes.** A fixed-roster trial raised the 90th percentile from 10 days to 44–47. The trial still concentrated too many missed games among starters.

The last new injury was February 4 for seed 17 and January 29 for seed 29, with over two months of games left. Teams ranged from 7 to 31 injuries in seed 17 and 10 to 30 in seed 29.

## What happens when players return

Basketball Index compares a player's first five games back with his last five before the absence. The first game and the five-game window both show a larger minutes drop after a longer absence. The source uses 2025–26 data and drops flagged rotation exits. True shooting combines two-pointers, three-pointers and free throws. Changes in that column are in percentage points.

|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | 431 | −0.9% / −1.7% | 289 | −0.2% / +0.1% | +0.11 (−0.62 to +0.65) | +0.08 |
| 2 | 144 | −7.2% / −5.0% | 278 | +0.8% / +0.6% | −0.47 (−1.38 to +0.20) | −1.02 |
| 3–5 | 94 | −9.9% / −6.5% | 149 | +0.5% / +1.0% | −1.12 (−2.22 to +0.47) | −1.29 |
| 6–10 | 47 | −16.2% / −11.6% | 20 | −3.5% / −1.9% | +1.16 (−2.83 to +2.93) | −3.26 |
| 11–20 | 32 | −21.2% / −14.7% | 46 | −2.9% / −2.5% | −0.02 (−1.24 to +2.84) | −1.00 |
| 21+ | 7 | −29.9% / −28.8% | 14 | −6.5% / −7.4% | −0.46 (−2.72 to +2.15) | +0.13 |

The sim comparison uses actual starts and returns, and requires five appearances on each side for the same team, at least eight minutes per game in the prior window, and no new injury before the post-return window ends. The box-score changes are descriptive. Lineup changes, opponents and shooting variation also affect them.

The sim removes a player during the injury, then restores eligibility when its return date arrives. Its minutes allocation has no dedicated return ramp. Rust subtracts 0.08 points per unit from the in-game impact feature, capped at 4, and applies smaller or larger weights to specific features. In the shooting profile, each penalty point reduces two-point, three-point and free-throw accuracy by 0.18, 0.12 and 0.06 percentage points, respectively, before rounding and box-score adjustments. A point of rust penalty therefore has no fixed conversion to realized true shooting.

| Sim severity | Rust loaded at injury | Penalty before decay | Penalty at the first possible game if it falls on the return date, average staff | Calendar decay at one step a day, including return day |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Day to day | 3 | 0.24 | 0 | 1 step |
| Short | 8 | 0.64 | 0 | 2 steps |
| Medium | 16 | 1.28 | 0.08 | 3 steps |
| Long | 28 | 2.24 | 1.04 | 4 steps |
| Season ending | 45 | 3.60 | 2.40 | 6 steps |

The setting is called `rust_decay_per_game`, but the normal save advances health even on off days. On a team's game day the health function usually advances the player twice, once before the injury roll and once at the end of the day's health pass, both before box-score simulation. At average staff quality that takes off 15 rust units. Better recovery staff speed up decay further. The per-point penalty and cap are defined in `sim.py`, so editing the similarly named config field alone does not change the penalty. Across all 1,097 returns observed in the two regular seasons, **1,050 (95.7%) had zero rust penalty on the first appearance**. All 999 day-to-day or short-band returns were in that group. Of the 47 returns that carried a penalty, 46 had it for one appearance and one had it for two.

## How the comparison works

- Each simulated injury's scheduled time out comes from its start and return dates. The regular-season games missed count uses actual rostered team game dates within that interval, including the injury date, when the sandbox already excludes the player from the box score. Time spent without a roster spot is outside the denominator. A player traded midseason contributes exposure with both clubs. An event is attributed to the team where it began.
- The Basketball Index history gives counts of **logged spells** among players covered by its current-player panel. The recent seasons with the most records are used as a benchmark. Reporting coverage varies, including across adjacent years. A report may describe an injury without a missed game. One event in the sim always costs at least the injury game. The two counts therefore give a scale check rather than a frequency target to copy directly. The dates come from the [2023–24](https://pr.nba.com/2023-24-nba-regular-season-schedule/), [2024–25](https://pr.nba.com/2024-25-nba-regular-season-schedule/) and [2025–26](https://pr.nba.com/2025-26-nba-regular-season-schedule/) NBA schedules.
- The larger return-day file uses six-year recency weighting. The estimate credits a return on the day it is recorded and treats cases without a return as known to last at least as long as observed. I counted each source observation once when combining categories. Basketball Index removes offseason days. The sim schedules a fixed number of calendar days even across summer. The sim's entire duration is known at injury creation, while a historical return can be unobserved.
- Historical age and role benchmarks come from `injury_model_reference.json`. For simulated role rows I joined to Basketball Index's 2026–27 profiles by player ID to assign the same roles, which are based on possessions per game when the player dressed. The games-missed rates are weighted by rostered team games, scaled to 82. The historical figures use Basketball Index's own covered player-season exposure. The projections column is a second reference for the same matched players, with their simulation exposure weights.

## What changed in the parameter trial

I replayed the full 82-game schedule through the daily health function for seeds 17 and 29, holding opening rosters, projected minutes and staff fixed. The regular gameplay runs in this report include changes to those inputs during the season. Values below are the range across the two fixed-roster runs.

| Trial | New injuries per team | Games lost per 82 rostered games | Days out, 25th / 50th / 75th / 90th |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Current settings | 18.8 | 4.4–4.8 | 2 / 3 / 5 / 10 |
| Relax caps and keep current lengths | 28.8–30.2 | 6.9–7.2 | 2 / 3 / 5 / 15–19 |
| Relax caps and draw longer injuries, 0.021 base chance | 25.8–26.9 | 11.4–12.0 | 2 / 6 / 17–19 / 44–47 |
| Same lengths, 0.016 base chance | 20.3–20.5 | 8.9–9.1 | 2 / 6 / 15–16 / 44–45 |
| Same lengths, 0.013 base chance | 16.4–17.3 | 7.3–7.6 | 2 / 6 / 15–16 / 44–45 |

The 0.021 trial gets the league's time lost closer to the historical age-band rates. It also pushes starters to 15.8–16.7 missed games and leaves bench players at 4.6–4.7 and the five matched deep reserves at zero. It leaves 9.4–10.3% of events open at the last regular-season date. Those results argue for spreading the exposure across the roster and fitting the tail by injury type before adopting the trial settings. Removing caps alone raised repeat injuries by season end from 14.1% to 29.1% in the five shared injury groups. Repeats within 30 days only rose from 8.4% to 9.8%. That leaves room to test a recent-injury effect after the cap change.

## Proposed changes to test

The first trial removes the hard stop and changes how often the sim draws long injuries. The second adds a coach's return-to-play plan. Changing injury days and injury odds together matters because a player who is already out cannot draw another injury.

| Trial | Starting values | Expected effect |

| Distinguish body areas and illnesses | Start with body weights of 27% ankle/foot, 17% knee, 11% shoulder/arm/hand, 10% back/core, 8% hip/groin, 2% head/face, 6% lower leg, 6% thigh, and 13% illness. These are rounded starting weights from the two richer recent history seasons. Fit duration separately by area, with rare diagnoses pooled toward their area. The 21+ game ramp uses a smaller cut than the observed average because that group contains only seven returns. The strongest short-term signal is reduced minutes for absences of two games or more. Every true-shooting interval spans zero. The long-term file also distinguishes diagnoses and uses matched controls. For example, ankle injuries averaged 3% fewer possessions in the next year among the matched data, with a small one-year adjusted plus-minus change whose interval spans zero. This audit supports testing changes to availability and deployment before applying any persistent ability effect.

An additional interaction to check before choosing a base injury rate is how rest is carried between days. The daily save path calls the health simulator with a fresh last-game lookup. That makes each team's first game in the batch start with the helper's default two rest days, including real back-to-backs. Carrying actual rest forward would affect fatigue and injury risk, so it deserves its own comparison.

For the next full-save trial, compare late-season event counts, actual games lost by age and role, the duration tail, and repeat injuries before choosing parameters. Recheck the market-odds and real-stat scorecards with the same baselines when a candidate changes gameplay. No engine or configuration change was made in this audit.

## Verification and reproduction

The two 2026–27 runs used Boston as the user team, the soak's default legacy rating mode, and seeds 17 and 29. Each ran all 1,230 regular-season games through the existing soak's app action, with 82 standings games for every team and no postseason games in the audit. The wrapper observes the health call and resumes at the trade-deadline checkpoint. I matched all 1,130 generated events to the saved ledger, reconciled all 4,019 missing player-games with the engine's health counters, and verified that no injured player appeared in a box score for a game counted as missed. Neither save had an opening-night carry-in absence.

Run from the repository root with the existing virtual environment. The commands below write to a fresh replay directory.

```sh
inj_replay=$(mktemp -d output/inj-replay.XXXXXX)
PYTHONPATH=src:scripts .venv/bin/python output/inj-injuries-vs-bbi/run.py 17 --results "$inj_replay"
PYTHONPATH=src:scripts .venv/bin/python output/inj-injuries-vs-bbi/run.py 29 --results "$inj_replay"
PYTHONPATH=src:scripts .venv/bin/python output/inj-injuries-vs-bbi/analyze.py --results "$inj_replay"
PYTHONPATH=src:scripts .venv/bin/python output/inj-injuries-vs-bbi/tables.py --results "$inj_replay"
PYTHONPATH=src:scripts .venv/bin/python output/inj-injuries-vs-bbi/probe.py --results "$inj_replay"
```

The report uses the local Basketball Index extracts in `data/research/bbi/tools/`. Raw simulation observations, the opening save for the parameter checks, the two finished saves and the machine-readable results are in `output/inj-injuries-vs-bbi/`.

# NBA contract structure and option decisions, 2019–2026

**TL;DR**

- Most sampled mid-level deals with a known multi-year salary path rose 5% per year. Likely Bird re-signings were more varied, with flat and declining deals alongside raises.
- Player options were common in the sampled maximum deals and more common in extensions for high-scoring regulars. Standard options were in the last year. First-round rookie deals had team options in years three and four.
- Players kept 93 of 177 ordinary options. Clubs exercised 89 of 186 standard team options, 233 of 240 third-year rookie options and 188 of 231 fourth-year rookie options.
- A player’s predicted market salary alone got 21 of 41 later decisions right, one more than always keeping the option. For clubs, price and minutes together got 54 of 85 right versus 44 for the baseline.
- Keep third-year rookie options as the usual choice and use weak playing time to review fourth years. Treat the signing rates as targets for comparable deals, with missing terms and partial coverage shown below.

## 1. How new deals pay

The sample contains 1,370 deals from calendar 2019 through November 21, 2026. It combines first-round rookie signings, standard free-agent signings with reported years and dollars, and extension trackers. A new free-agent maximum and a maximum extension are separate events. An extension’s first year is the first added salary season. The same player can appear more than once on different deals. A player option adds the final season at the contract salary. An early termination clause lets the player leave before the last season of a five-year deal. Anthony Davis had an early termination clause in his 2020 Lakers max. Fred VanVleet’s 2023 Houston max had a team option, a rare arrangement in this sample. A non-guaranteed year can be cut under its own terms. Aron Baynes’s 2020 Toronto deal was first reported with a team option and later clarified as a non-guaranteed second year. The dedicated second-round pick exception also produces a team option in year three or four under the 2023 agreement. Its take-up is outside the measured exception rates above. ## 4. Rules checked on later seasons

The fit used 2019–2024 to choose thresholds, then tested those same thresholds on 2025 and 2026. The baseline always makes the more common decision in the training years. Thresholds were selected by averaging the fraction of exercises and declines correctly called, so rare declines get a say. The held-out table shows the count right for easier comparison. The market estimate is the median annual salary of the nine closest unrestricted signings from the training years, scaled to the cap when the decision is due. Similarity uses age, availability, minutes, scoring and the NBA’s box-score contribution measure. The estimate excludes the target season and the player’s own deals, including repeat signings. It has limited evidence on years of security, guarantees and the team’s alternative offers.

| Decision and fitted rule | Correct in 2025 | Correct in 2026 | Both years |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Player baseline, keep | 10/20 | 10/21 | 20/41 |
| Player leaves at estimated market pay of 130% of the option | 11/20 | 10/21 | 21/41 |
| Player leaves at 1,000 prior minutes per 82 team games | 16/20 | 12/21 | 28/41 |
| Standard team baseline, decline | 18/33 | 26/52 | 44/85 |
| Standard team picks up at estimated market pay of 90% of the option | 19/33 | 31/52 | 50/85 |
| Standard team picks up at 130% of the option, or 105% for a player with 500 prior minutes per 82 team games | 20/33 | 34/52 | 54/85 |
| Live rookie fourth-year baseline, pick up | 23/25 | 19/26 | 42/51 |
| Live rookie fourth-year pickup for a top-five pick or 1,500 prior minutes per 82 team games | 13/25 | 16/26 | 29/51 |

**Player options.** Treat the price gap and regular playing time as inputs to a choice. Austin Reaves’s $14.9 million option in 2026 was low relative to the competition for his next deal, and the market rule called his decline correctly. Kyrie Irving’s $44 million option in 2025 broke the market-only rule. He accepted about $119 million over three years from Dallas while recovering from a torn knee ligament, giving up some annual pay for several years of protection. Lonzo Ball, who missed the previous season, kept his $21.4 million in 2024. Adding age, availability or team winning percentage to the market rule gave no held-out improvement. The injury variant added one correct decision. The injury count is available mainly in the final three seasons, so its coefficient is weak evidence. [Reaves terms](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/06/lakers-austin-reaves-agree-to-four-year-max-deal.html) · [Irving terms](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/06/kyrie-irving-to-decline-option-re-sign-with-mavs-on-three-year-deal.html) · [Ball decision](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2024/05/bulls-lonzo-ball-exercises-2024-25-player-option.html)

**Standard team options.** Price relative to replacement and recent minutes worked best together. Minutes alone got 45 of 85 right. Adding age to the market rule got 51, team winning percentage got 52, and injury games got 51. Houston declined VanVleet’s $44.9 million option on June 25, 2025, then secured him on a two-year deal. Brooklyn declined Day’Ron Sharpe’s $6.25 million option and brought him back on a new deal, reported June 29, 2026. A rule that predicts option pickup can miss a good player whom the team wants on a different contract. Let a credible new deal compete with the price of exercising. [VanVleet return](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/06/rockets-re-signing-fred-vanvleet-on-two-year-deal.html) · [Sharpe return](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/06/nets-to-sign-dayron-sharpe-josh-minott-to-two-year-deals.html)

**Rookie options.** Treat the observed draft-slot and minutes rates as starting points. The fitted fourth-year rule found seven of nine live declines but would have dropped 20 of the 42 players teams kept. It called Topić correctly and would have dropped Indiana’s Jarace Walker in 2025 after 1,187 minutes. Keep most live fourth years and use low minutes as a review signal, especially for later picks with no established rotation role. Treat waivers as their own roster events when the option is still attached. ## Coverage and handoff

- The signing sample measures public records with reported years and money. One-year minimums can be harder to distinguish from prorated deals, and options removed by waiver, extension or renegotiation can go unreported. Exception labels use named spending reports. The likely Bird group uses a five-year re-signing or a reported return after two salary seasons with the same team. This is evidence of eligibility and can miss Bird rights carried in a trade or include a return paid from cap room. The opt-in and pickup rates are tracker rates, with older standard team decisions particularly likely to be incomplete.
- Salary schedules use matches against the reported total, the existing Basketball Reference contract snapshot and other historical salary observations, or an original contract report or published scale. Traded or amended contracts, incentives, and projected max extensions leave many paths unresolved. The research file keeps the options and salary path unknowns explicit. Existing Basketball Reference research was reused locally. Automated acquisition from sites whose terms restrict it was excluded. [Historical salary research data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Mr-Bridge/nba-salary-cap-contracts-2016-2026) · [2026 salary ledger](https://www.basketball-reference.com/contracts/players.html)
- Run `python3 scripts/research/contract_structure_fit.py` from the worktree to regenerate the holdouts. The script reads the checked-in evidence and needs only the Python standard library. The JSON includes the row-level facts, confidence and timing notes, denominators, sources, and fitted results. The shared board is marked for coordinator review.

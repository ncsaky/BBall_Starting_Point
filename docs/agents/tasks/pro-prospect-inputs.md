# PRO · Rookie ratings and first-season evidence

**The 2026 rookies enter with veteran-style skill estimates.** Their startup records were built without the estimator’s rookie skill setting. Darryn Peterson also gets assigned 54.1% involvement, higher than any of the 99 recent rookies in the comparison.

**Test the existing rookie skill setting in the startup builder first.** In the prototype, Peterson moves from No. 15 to No. 84 and Cameron Boozer from No. 64 to No. 264. Cooper Flagg’s rating stays 64.0 and he moves from No. 120 to No. 102 as other players move below him.

**Treat involvement separately.** The fictional class shows a high-usage tail in one save. Its typical involvement is close to or below recent rookies, depending on draft band.

## 1. Estimated inputs against first-season evidence

Each percentile cell reads **observed 2023–25 / installed 2026 / fictional 2027**. The sample is the current player universe matched by player ID to the first NBA season after each draft. Basketball Index’s Stable True Usage% capture supplies the minutes filter. I required 500 minutes and an observed usage value. Later debut seasons and rookies missing from the current universe or evidence capture are outside this comparison. Second-round results describe players who cleared that minutes threshold.

Historical inputs use same-season raw Basketball Index values and the repository’s reviewed metric directions and weights. I ranked each composite against that season’s Basketball Index pool of players with 500 minutes. For the estimates, I ranked each input against 638 startup records outside the 2026 class. Skill-group figures average available input percentiles for each player, then take the draft-band median. I compared only mapped dimensions where an observed counterpart exists. These are relative league ranks from different pools, and the Basketball Index metrics capture what players did in their roles. Both differences limit how precisely the gaps can calibrate underlying skill.

## 2. Where the gap starts

| Pipeline step | What the comparison showed |
|---|---|
| Startup record builder | `scripts/build_player_inputs.py` calls `InputEstimator.estimate_universe`, which uses the default `prospect=False`. Peterson, Boozer, Mara and Wilson already have records with all 94 inputs. The builder path reproduced every one of their 64 technique values, 12 physical values and involvement. The runtime installer skips those records. |
| Rookie skill setting | Holding the old traits constant and switching `prospect=True` changed all 64 technique values for each of those four players. All 12 physical values and involvement matched. In the estimator, most technique anchors drop by six points before other adjustments. Free throws drop by three. Peterson’s ball-control grade moved from 67.38 to 61.70 and Boozer’s from 53.92 to 47.90. |
| Old ratings used as inputs | On the startup path, setting old traits to neutral moved Peterson’s ball-control grade from 67.38 to 62.00 and Boozer’s from 53.92 to 50.00. Replacing just the four 2026 test profiles with their neutral-trait builds moves Peterson to No. 170 and Boozer to No. 298. That is a sensitivity check. The old traits supply player-specific information, and their wide influence contributes to the initial grades. |
| Involvement anchors and scale map | Both estimator paths start at 62% for a creator, handler or playmaker role, and 42% for other roles, on the estimator’s pre-map scale. Peterson’s old rim-pressure and handling traits move him from 62.0% to 67.3%. The map converts those two values to 40.7% and 54.1%, respectively. A 5.3-point raw lift turns into a 13.4-point lift on the usage scale. For Boozer the full path takes 42.0% to 45.2% before mapping, then to 31.1%. The map is monotone and only acts on involvement and on-ball tendency. Skills and physicals already have their high values before this step. |
| Trait adapter and card | The adapter ranks the inputs, combines the resulting percentiles and ranks the composite against the league before assigning a derived trait. Peterson’s handling composite was 93.4, which mapped to the 98.8th percentile among league composites. Mara’s rim-deterrence composite went from 79.8 to the 95.0th percentile. The card also uses direct inputs and involvement. The adapter can accentuate clustered high grades, while the raw cohort gaps are visible before it runs. |
| Fictional player generation | The current fictional rookie path estimates from similar players using generator ability, position, old traits and body information. It uses a separate branch from the real 2026 startup build. Its sample starts higher in some skills and lower in others. Its neighbor reference can include real 2026 startup records, so a future rebuild should recheck this branch with the corrected records. |

In the startup league, Mara ranks No. 65 overall, Harper No. 76 and Wilson No. 94. Mara’s height and reach estimates are around the 99th percentile. Wilson’s recovery estimate is near the 97th percentile. Harper belongs to the 2025 observed class. His on-court usage is part of the historical comparison, and his installed involvement is 37.4% after blending. One of his passing-execution grades is at the 98.5th percentile of the model input pool. That skill has no mapped Basketball Index counterpart here. Flagg’s installed involvement is 37.7% after blending, versus 39.2% observed.

## 3. Smallest fix and expected ranks

Use the existing rookie technique setting at startup for the 53 real 2026 entrants with no completed NBA season. Keep eligible evidence overlays and the old player-specific trait adjustments in the build. The prototype swaps the 64 technique estimates only. The ranks below come from rederiving the 691 startup cards with the current league calibration and display tables held fixed. Ratings in parentheses are the displayed overall scores.

| Player | Current | Rookie technique only | Creator involvement only | Both |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Darryn Peterson | #15 (80.5) | #84 (66.2) | #50 (71.0) | #236 (53.9) |
| Cameron Boozer | #64 (69.9) | #264 (52.9) | #56 (69.9) | #256 (52.9) |
| Cooper Flagg | #120 (64.0) | #102 (64.0) | #108 (64.0) | #101 (64.0) |

The rookie anchors bring top-five defense technique from the 70.9th to the 44.7th percentile (48.9 observed), passing reads from 80.3 to 61.6 (63.7 observed), and shooting from 73.6 to 57.2 (38.3 observed). This is a useful starting point for the builder fix. The same switch leaves top-five post craft high (65.3 versus 52.4) and takes second-round handling low (22.4 versus 47.3). Check those groups by draft year before setting final anchors. The expected ranks describe these prototype settings. A full rebuild would also refit the league tables.

For the independent involvement test, I shifted post-map probabilities on the log-odds scale for the 21 role-tagged 2026 creators. Their median starts at 48.6%, compared with 30.9% for 56 historical rookies grouped with the same current role labels. The single shift preserves the ordering within that role group and takes Peterson to 35.8%. The picks 6–14 median remains at the 64.6th percentile after the combined change, against the 30.0th percentile observed. Validate the role-based shift on held-out seasons before adopting it. The magnitude of the change in Peterson’s card is also worth checking against both game scorecards when testing implementation.

## PRO-2 results

The startup builder now uses the rookie technique estimates for real 2026 draftees without a completed NBA season. The change is limited to technique. The existing Basketball Index overlay and measured-tendency steps still run in their original order. The proposed involvement shift is a separate task.

Peterson fell from No. 15 to No. 83 overall and Boozer from No. 64 to No. 255. The replay stayed at 18 of 18 league measures within range. The average scoring error increased by 0.002 points per 36 minutes.

### Rebuilt input files

The JSON and gzip contain 691 players. The 53 entrants changed on all 64 technique grades each, for 3,392 changed values and records. All 61,562 other input records were identical, including the 53 entrants' physical tools and tendencies and all inputs for the other 638 players. Player and file metadata matched, the gzip decodes to the exact JSON, and the tendency scale map was byte-identical.

A full build with the current universe picked up the separate measurement update for 11 players and related estimates. For the published artifacts, I ran the same builder with the universe snapshot from the parent of `741d768` and current evidence. The 53 entrant records and their 795 legacy traits match the current universe exactly. All 3,392 published grades and their supporting records also match the live-universe build. The old snapshot was used only for artifact generation. The scorecards used the live universe in both runs.

All changed records passed strict validation individually. The strict whole-file validator rejects both artifacts because 890 existing tendency probability rows differ from a sum of 1 by about 0.000001, above the 0.000000001 tolerance. Those rows are identical in both artifacts.

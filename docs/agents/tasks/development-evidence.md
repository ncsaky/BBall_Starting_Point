# NBA player development evidence, 2004–05 through 2025–26

## TL;DR

- Real players made their biggest gains at 19–21. Average impact peaked around 25–26, and individual seasons could move sharply in either direction.
- Among players who kept playing regular minutes, decline averaged about half an impact point a year in the early 30s and nearly a full point in the late 30s. Established stars held up better at 33–35.
- Early impact and draft slot helped predict top-25 and top-50 seasons. For the very top, the forecasts were weaker and long shots still broke through.
- About one player a year reached the top five for the first time. About two of the five spots changed hands each season, including returning players.
- Sixteen players first made the top 25 at 27–30 after appearing in the NBA at 23. Twenty-three of 150 top-ten picks fell short of both a top-50 year and three seasons of regular minutes in their first seven years.

## 1. How players improve and age

Basketball Reference's **Box Plus/Minus (BPM)** estimates how many points a player adds per 100 possessions above league average from the box score. **Value Over Replacement Player (VORP)** estimates season-long contribution above a replacement player and gives credit for more playing time. BPM is the main growth and ranking measure. VORP identifies established stars for the aging comparison and provides a check on the top five. The main panel has 11,059 player-seasons from 2004–05 through 2025–26. Earlier seasons back to 1995–96 are used to identify prior entries and NBA debuts. Year labels use the season's ending year. [2004–05 advanced stats](https://www.basketball-reference.com/leagues/NBA_2005_advanced.html) · [2025–26 advanced stats](https://www.basketball-reference.com/leagues/NBA_2026_advanced.html) · [Basketball Reference methodology](https://www.basketball-reference.com/about/bpm2.html)

Adding each year's mean change from age 19 onward and smoothing over three ages gives a **peak at 26**. The older fitting seasons alone also gave 26. With a 750-minute cut the peak was 25, and with 1,500 minutes it was 26. The age-24-to-27 stretch is fairly flat. The same-player comparisons avoid comparing entirely different players from one age to the next. The set of players who qualify still changes with age. At 30, 242 of 323 starting qualifiers met the cut again the next year. At 35, 54 of 84 did. That lost playing time is another part of aging beyond the change in impact per possession.

The star comparison uses a top-25 VORP finish **one season before** the starting season. That measures a player's recent standing without picking the stars from whichever players posted the highest BPM at the start of the change being measured. At 33–35, the stars still playing regular minutes lost about half a point less per year than other returning qualifiers in both measures. At 30–32 they declined faster in both measures. The oldest star sample includes repeat seasons from a few long careers. A blanket rule that all stars age more slowly would miss those differences.

**Held-out test.** Fit average changes in seven broad age bands using target seasons 2006–2020, then forecast each pair ending in 2021–2026. Lower average absolute error is better.

| Rule | Average absolute error across 1,211 held-out pairs |
|---|---:|
| Repeat last year's BPM | 1.37 |
| Add average change for the age band | 1.33 |
| Add average change for the age band and starting level | 1.29 |

The middle-80% band estimated from the fitting sample covered 75.9% of held-out outcomes. The decline of players grouped by a strong starting season includes regression toward their usual level and shifts in role or health. It would be a mistake to subtract that entire amount from their underlying skills every year.

**Basketball Index check.** LEBRON blends box-score evidence with how teams perform with and without a player. The 2010–2026 dashboard matched 8,731 of 8,733 rows to the public panel. Two same-name Chris Johnson rows could not be assigned confidently. The progression capture agreed with the overlapping LEBRON values within the dashboard's rounding for the middle 80% of rows. Using the same matched pairs, both measures put the smoothed peak at 26. In LEBRON, average gains at 19, 20 and 21 were +1.02, +0.79 and +0.52. The dashboard and progression capture are two views of the same Basketball Index statistic.

# NBA free agency and the 2026 offseason

**TL;DR**\
The NBA lets teams talk to their own free agents first, opens talks with everyone on June 30, and makes most deals official on July 6. The game can already change rosters in June.\
A qualifying offer gives a team the right to match another team's signed offer to an eligible young player. The game skips that decision and usually keeps good young players automatically.\
Bird rights let a team spend above the salary cap to keep its own players. Holding those rights uses up spending room until the player signs or the team gives up the rights. The game gives the rights without charging for them.\
The league gives teams specific ways to spend above the cap and imposes firm ceilings after certain moves. The game mostly lets teams shop up to the tax line, which is where a bill starts in the NBA.\
Give the player four main stops: decide who to keep, make offers, finalize deals and answer matches, then set the roster. Stop between those dates only for a decision that needs their attention.

## 1. What the NBA does

### 2026 calendar

“Free agency” begins when a contract ends and a player can negotiate a new one. An **unrestricted free agent** can choose any team that can legally sign him. A **restricted free agent** still needs his old team to pass or let its clock expire before he can join a new team on an **offer sheet**, a signed outside contract subject to matching. A **moratorium** is the short period when teams can negotiate most deals but have to wait to sign them. An **extension** adds years to an existing contract.

### Restricted free agency

**Who can be restricted.** The previous team must send a valid **qualifying offer**, a signed offer of a one-year contract that the player may take, as well as the way the team obtains its matching right. Eligible players include first-rounders finishing the fourth season of their original rookie scale contract, other expiring veterans with at most three credited years of NBA service as of June 30, and players completing two-way contracts. A first-rounder's unexercised third- or fourth-year scale option leads to unrestricted status and a special limit on what the old team can pay. Age and playing quality do not determine eligibility. The NBA's public 2026 options article mentions a 15-day NBA roster test for two-way players. That language is in the 2017 agreement and is absent from 2023 Article XI §4(b), which also expressly covers completed two-ways. Use the signed 2023 text. A two-way is a contract to serve both the NBA team and its developmental-league affiliate. [CBA Art. XI §§1(e), 4 and Art. VII §6(n)(3)](https://nbpa.com/cba/) · [NBA options explainer](https://www.nba.com/news/2026-free-agency-options-and-qualifying-offers)

| Qualifying offer | Amount and decision |
|---|---|
| First-rounder who finished year four | Fourth-year salary plus the percentage for his draft slot in his draft class's rookie scale. Apply the **starter criteria** of 41 starts or 2,000 regular-season minutes in year four or as an average over years three and four. A pick outside the top nine who qualifies gets the No. 9 offer based on a 120%-of-scale contract. A top-14 pick who misses gets the lesser of his own normal figure and the No. 15 offer based on a 120%-of-scale contract. Jalen Duren's 2026 offer was about $9.6M after meeting the starter criteria. [CBA Art. XI §1(e)(ii) and Exhibit B](https://nbpa.com/cba/) · [Hoops Rumors on Duren's starter criteria](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/10/jalen-duren-reaches-starter-criteria-increasing-qo.html) |
| Other standard-contract player | The greater of 135% of previous salary (125% for the remaining pre-July 2023 contract cases) or the player's new minimum plus $200,000. For a second-round or undrafted player with two or three years of service meeting the starter criteria, also compare the No. 21 offer at 100% of that rookie scale and take it if higher. Apply the CBA bonus rules to the previous-salary route. [CBA Art. XI §1(e)(iv)–(v)](https://nbpa.com/cba/) |
| Completing a two-way | Usually one year at two-way pay, with about $91,000 protected for 2026–27. After a two-year deal, two successive years with the same team (including the specified trade/claim continuity case), or when a player cannot sign another two-way with that team, offer one year at his standard minimum with about $109,000 protected. The protection figures are the respective $75,000 and $90,000 bases scaled by the cap. A qualifying offer on a standard deal other than this two-way graduate route is fully protected for skill and injury or illness. [CBA Art. XI §1(e), Art. II §11(c)](https://nbpa.com/cba/) |
| Optional maximum qualifying offer | A team tendering the ordinary offer to a first-rounder after year four can also offer five fully protected seasons at his applicable opening max and 8% annual raises based on first-year salary, with no option, early termination or bonus. He can take either offer while open. This also raises the minimum length of an outside sheet. [CBA Art. XI §§4(a)(ii), 5(b)](https://nbpa.com/cba/) |

**Player and incumbent choices.** They can agree on a different new deal at any time it can legally be signed. The player can accept the one-year offer, seek a signed outside sheet, or wait. Accepting the one-year offer can lead to unrestricted free agency after the season, particularly for a fourth-year player who now passes the service limit. A still-eligible player can be restricted again. On the standard one-year deal, if he would finish it with Full or Early Bird rights, he generally can veto a trade unless he waived that right in the contract. If he consents to a trade, those Bird rights drop to Non-Bird for the resulting free agency. A team can pull the offer through July 13 and keep its ordinary Bird rights. From July 14 it needs written consent and withdrawal also renounces the player's rights. Expiration on October 1 only ends acceptance, unless extended by written notice before the deadline, up to March 1. A team can give up matching after the acceptance window, surrendering the player's rights. If no outside sheet is given by March 1 and the player never signs with the incumbent that season, it can send the same offer again by June 29. [CBA Art. XI §§4(c), 5(a), 5(o), 5(q), Art. VII §§4(g), 8(b)](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**The outside sheet.** The player and one outside team sign a standard deal with at least two seasons excluding any option year, or at least three if the incumbent made both qualifying offers. The outside maximum is four years and ordinary annual changes are at most 5% of year-one salary. The CBA requires the bidding team to have enough **Room** when the sheet is signed and to preserve it until resolution. The agreement uses Room to mean either space below the salary cap or an applicable exception. For example, an eligible mid-level exception can fund a sheet that fits its dollars, years and apron conditions. A minimum contract or a trade credit cannot be used as an all-purpose source of bidding room. One outstanding sheet per player, revocable only by written agreement of all three parties. A sheet can specify permitted fixed pay, a signing bonus within the 10% offer-sheet limit, protection, options and a permitted trade bonus. The terms the incumbent must match include length, fixed pay and timing, allowable contract amendments, league honors bonuses and incentives likely for both teams. An incentive specific to the new team and unlikely for the old team is outside those principal terms. An optional physical follows special examination and notice clocks. Terms aimed at paying differently solely because a particular team matches cannot force the match price up. [CBA Art. I §1(kkk), Art. II §12(a), Art. VII §5(b), Art. IX §1, Art. XI §5(b), (e)–(f), (k)–(l), (n), (s) and Exhibit G](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Arenas rule and backloaded pay.** This rule applies by **one or two years of service**, including a second-rounder, undrafted player or other eligible free agent in that service band. An ordinary first-rounder finishing his four-year scale deal is outside the band. A first-rounder whose scale option was declined is unrestricted. An offer can start at most at the full non-taxpayer mid-level amount ($15.044M in 2026–27), with the normal 5% first-year-based change in year two. If both years are at their permitted maximum, year three can jump to the salary possible in year three had the deal started at the player's regular max with ordinary raises. Year four may change by at most 4.5% of year three. The jump requires full protection all years and no bonuses. The bidder must have room for the average and books that same average each year if it gets the player. A matching team normally books the actual year-by-year figures, with the option to flat-average if its current team salary plus the average fits under the cap. For example, at the 2026 cap, a three-year offer could pay about $15.044M, $15.796M and $45.364M. The bidder would reserve about $25.401M. The incumbent could face a $45.364M third-year charge. That jump is often called a “poison pill.” The other use of that phrase refers to trade accounting for a first-rounder with a signed rookie extension before it starts, a different rule. [CBA Art. XI §5(d), Art. VII §§6(j), 8(g), Art. II §7](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Matching.** The incumbent must be able to pay the first-year charge including unlikely bonuses with room, applicable Bird rights or the minimum exception. The special two-year-service Early Bird rule can match an Arenas sheet. The separate non-taxpayer mid-level matching route in Article VII §6(e)(5) has its own eligibility and available-balance tests, including the first-year amount and the first-apron limit when that exception is used. Apply the already-triggered hard cap as well. After the moratorium, a sheet received **before noon** is due **11:59 p.m. the next day**. A sheet received **at or after noon** is due **11:59 p.m. two days later**. For example, Tuesday 11 a.m. is due Wednesday night, Tuesday 1 p.m. is due Thursday night. The often-used “24 hours” shorthand refers to the July 6-to-7 matching stretch and is unsafe as a general countdown. If matched, the old team signs on the principal terms. If declined or time runs out, the new team signs on all terms, subject to any physical. No picks or cash are owed for passing. No amendment for a year. A match blocks a trade without the player's consent for a year and blocks any trade to the bidder for a year even with consent. No match-and-trade or side payment for choosing whether to match. [CBA Art. XI §5(c), (g)–(m), (r)–(u), Art. VII §6(b)(3), §6(e)(5)](https://nbpa.com/cba/) · [NBA CBA 101, Free Agency](https://cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2024/11/2024-25-CBA-101.pdf)

### Bird rights, holds and spending room

The **salary cap** is the league's basic payroll limit. It is **soft** because exceptions let teams exceed it for specified moves. **Cap room** is the unused amount under that line after all required charges. The **luxury tax** is a bill triggered by a separate calculation above a higher line. An **apron** is a payroll threshold that limits certain roster moves. A **hard cap** is a ceiling triggered by making a specified move, including a move that uses an exception. A team that has triggered one must stay within it for the covered year. The same payroll can produce different numbers for cap room, tax and aprons. [CBA Art. VII §§2, 4–6](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Bird rights** are three exceptions for re-signing a team's own free agent. Count seasons covered by contracts, including a partial first season where applicable. Trades preserve the clock. For Full Bird, a waiver assignment only preserves the three-season chain if it is in the first of those seasons. For Early Bird, waiver claims can carry the chain across the two seasons. Clearing waivers and signing as a free agent generally starts a new chain, with exceptions in the definitions, including signing with the eventual prior team in the first qualifying season. A Bird signing by itself does not impose a hard cap, and it cannot break one already in force. Raises are percentage points of the first-year salary added to each successive year. For example, with 8% raises, $20M becomes $21.6M and then $23.2M. [CBA Art. I §1(t), (rr), (yy), Art. VII §§2(e), 5(a), 6(b), Art. IX §1](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

| Rights | First-year salary ceiling within the exception | New deal length and annual change | Usual cap hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full Bird, three qualifying seasons | Player's maximum | Up to five seasons, up to 8% | 190% of previous pay below the previous year's estimated average, 150% at or above it. After year four of the rookie scale, use 300% below or 250% at or above. |
| Early Bird, two qualifying seasons | Greater of 175% of final-year regular salary plus corresponding bonuses, or 105% of the prior year's league **average player salary** (use the CBA estimate before audit), subject to player max | Two to four seasons, with at least two before any option year. Up to 8%. | 130% of previous salary. Team may step down to Non-Bird and its cheaper hold. |
| Non-Bird, other incumbent veterans | Greatest of 120% of prior regular salary plus corresponding bonuses, 120% of his applicable minimum, or required qualifying-offer pay if restricted, subject to player max | Up to four seasons, up to 5% | 120% of previous salary. |
| Outside team | Actual cap room or an eligible exception, subject to player max | Usually up to four seasons, up to 5%. The chosen exception may allow fewer years. | No incumbent free-agent hold. A signed offer sheet reserves the bidder's Room. |

**Cap holds** are temporary charges in the cap-room calculation. For the usual player holds above, prior pay includes signing-bonus allocation and bonuses actually earned. The hold is bounded by the individual max and the applicable unreimbursed minimum. Players coming off minimum pay typically hold at that minimum charge, and expiring two-way players hold at the rookie standard minimum. A declined first-round option also bounds the hold. On a restricted player, add the applicable qualifying offer, maximum qualifying offer and matching charges only as required by the CBA's greater-of calculation. For ordinary cap room, compare the player hold, the outstanding standard qualifying offer (or maximum qualifying offer if both were tendered), and any match notice. The two-way version of a qualifying offer is excluded from that ordinary cap-room comparison. For apron salary, exclude the ordinary player hold but include the greater applicable qualifying offer, maximum qualifying offer or match notice amount. The signed contract replaces the hold. Signing elsewhere or **renouncing** the player, which means giving up the relevant rights, removes it. The team can no longer use his Bird exception. Limited undo is possible within two business days after a matched sheet if the renunciations were made to fund that sheet and the payroll conditions allow restoring them. [CBA Art. VII §§2(e)(1), 4(a)(2), 4(d), 4(g), 5, 6(b), 6(n)(3)](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Other charges and order of moves.** An unsigned first-round pick carries 120% of the scale when drafted, subject to the agreement's overseas and non-signing rules. From July 1 before opening day, charge a rookie minimum for each empty slot below 12 chargeable players. Signed players, qualifying free-agent holds, pending sheets and applicable first-round holds count toward those 12. Unused exceptions such as the mid-level can also occupy room until used, lost or renounced. For example, $155M in other charges plus a $19M Full Bird hold on a $10M sub-average expiring veteran leaves no room below the $164.961M cap. Giving up that hold can open almost $10M before slot and exception charges, while keeping it can let the team re-sign him at a lower number and use the remaining space afterward. Show before and after for the actual order of moves. [CBA Art. VII §§4(a), 4(d)–(g), 6(n)](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

### Spending tools and aprons

For 2026–27 the official cap is **$164.961M**, the team salary floor **$148.465M**, the tax line **$200.428M**, first apron **$209.015M**, second apron **$221.686M**. The **salary floor** is the minimum team payroll, normally 90% of the cap. Falling short on opening day creates a payroll charge for the shortfall, a player distribution and loss of eligibility for tax distributions that year. The salary cap and tax are established annually from league revenues under the CBA, with smoothing and a 10% annual increase limit. [NBA 2026–27 cap announcement](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-salary-cap-2026-27-season) · [CBA Art. VII §2(a)–(e)](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

A **mid-level exception** is a preset budget for one or more contracts. An eligible team can split the budget among players. The three forms are alternatives, and a team spending actual cap room cannot also take the regular mid-level and bi-annual budgets. Teams with the first two spending paths below can still owe tax. The signing raises for the four fixed budgets below are up to 5% of the starting salary. Full/room mid-level and bi-annual can also be used for eligible trade or waiver acquisitions under remaining-term limits, effective since 2024–25. Most unused signing budgets begin shrinking with the days left on January 10, with the CBA suspending the reduction for use between then and the trade deadline and for matching sheets. [CBA Art. VII §§2(e), 5(a), 6(d)–(g), 6(n)](https://nbpa.com/cba/) · [NBA cap announcement](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-salary-cap-2026-27-season)

**Hard-cap events in 2026–27.**

- **First apron.** Use the full non-taxpayer mid-level subject to the short/small reclassification rule, use bi-annual, receive a signed-and-traded player, sign during the regular season a player waived during that season whose prior salary exceeded the full mid-level, take back more salary under the expanded trade exception, or use a standard trade credit after the regular season cutoff for the credit's originating period. The transitional trade exception in the CBA table was for 2023–24. [CBA Art. VII §§2(e)(2)–(4), table A–G, 6(j)](https://nbpa.com/cba/)
- **Second apron.** Use the taxpayer mid-level, combine outgoing player salaries as one source of matching salary in a trade (**aggregation**), send trade cash, or acquire someone using a trade exception based on an outgoing signed-and-traded contract. A team above the second apron has no mid-level route and can't aggregate to match a bigger incoming contract. A team above the first apron already cannot receive a signed-and-traded player. Sending out a free agent by sign-and-trade is a different question from receiving him or spending the credit he created. [CBA Art. VII §§2(e)(2)–(4), table C and H–K, 6(j), 8(e)](https://nbpa.com/cba/)
- **Duration and what counts.** Apply the relevant apron payroll immediately after the move, then keep the triggered ceiling for the rest of the cap year. The tightest applicable ceiling wins. Certain post-regular-season trades through June 30 can impose the same ceiling in the next cap year too, with option and award assumptions in §2(e)(3). Apron payroll accounts for unlikely performance bonuses and some required offers. It strips out ordinary free-agent and empty-roster holds. Above an apron without a hard cap, a team still can retain players using eligible Bird rights and sign minimum players. A second-apron finish also freezes the first-round pick seven drafts out, with possible end-of-round penalty under the repeat-year test. [CBA Art. VII §§2(e)–(f)](https://nbpa.com/cba/) · [NBA executives' apron Q&A](https://www.nba.com/news/qa-nba-executives-break-down-the-second-apron-competitive-balance-and-more)

### Other deals and contracts

**Sign-and-trade.** The incumbent, the player and the receiving team agree that the incumbent signs its free agent and immediately sends him to the new team. The player must have finished the prior season on that incumbent's roster. Do it before the regular season, for three or four seasons with at least three before any option year, and with first-season pay fully protected against lack of skill. Ordinary outside-team raise limits apply. The acquiring team needs Room for first-year salary plus unlikely bonuses and becomes hard-capped at the first apron. The outgoing contract cannot be signed using the full non-taxpayer or room mid-level. The 30% fifth-year performance max above the normal 25% cannot be sent this way. A physical can be a trade condition. In a qualifying over-cap Full/Early Bird raise, **base-year compensation** means the outgoing team counts the greater of prior salary and half the new first-year salary for trade matching. The receiver counts the full new salary. For example, a qualifying jump from $12M to $30M counts as $15M outgoing and $30M incoming. This can require a third team or cap room. An already-matched sheet cannot be converted to a sign-and-trade. [CBA Art. VII §§2(e), 5(b), 6(j)(5), 8(e), Art. XI §5(m)](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Options and guarantees.** A player or team option is a choice to add the contracted option year. An **early termination option** lets the player end the existing deal early after at least four seasons. These choices are written into the deal and cannot depend on later performance. Standard team/player options are generally a single season at the end, with pay terms constrained by the previous year. First-round scale contracts have two pre-set team options with earlier exercise deadlines. A **guarantee** is pay still owed if a player is released under the protection in his contract. A **partial guarantee** protects only a stated amount, which can grow on a negotiated date. The specific protection can depend on why he was cut, including injury, and the default January guarantee applies to the current season. A **buyout** is a negotiated giveback in connection with release. The adjusted salary owed after a release remains on the team's cap as **dead salary**, subject to claims, the CBA's offset for certain later earnings, and any permitted stretch election. [CBA Art. II §§3(i), 4, Art. VII §§4(a), 7(d), Art. VIII §1, Art. XII §§1–5, Art. XXVII, Exhibit A](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Waivers and stretch.** Releasing a player starts a 48-hour claim period. A claim takes the existing contract and requires eligible room/exception and a legal roster. The lowest standings win, using previous-season standings from the end of the season through November 30. Head-to-head breaks ties where possible, then a coin toss. If claimed, the contract changes teams. Otherwise the player can negotiate a new one and the old team keeps any protected obligation. Claiming teams typically cannot trade him for 30 days. The salary-cap **stretch** can spread eligible remaining dead salary over twice the remaining seasons plus one, e.g. $30M for two years becomes $6M for five. Elections in July/August include that cap year's charge. September through June leave current-year money unchanged and stretch future charges. Both termination and election must precede September 1 of the original final season. A club seeking the July/August treatment must leave time for waivers to clear by August 31. The future waived-player charge may not exceed 15% of the election-year cap, and the team cannot reacquire him before the July 1 after the original final season, including any option year. The stretch affects cap allocation. The cash payment schedule is governed separately by the mandatory stretch provisions and any allowed agreement. [CBA Art. II §4(k), Art. VII §§4(a), 6, 7(d)(5)–(6), Art. XXVII §5, Exhibit A §§5.03–5.06](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Two-way and Exhibit 10.** A team gets three two-way places in addition to its 14–15 standard contracts in-season. Two-way pay is half the rookie minimum, about $678,882 for a full 2026–27 season. It is prorated after opening and normally outside NBA team salary. A deal is one or two seasons with no options. A player generally cannot enter an agreement that would cover a season with four or more years of service, with a narrow one-year exception for certain players credited a full season without appearing in a game. Maximum three cap years with the same team. Up to 50 NBA active-list games in a full season, prorated when applicable. An additional team-wide count of 90 two-way active-list slots applies in games with fewer than 15 standard players. Convert to the standard minimum before the last regular-season game for playoff eligibility, or negotiate a permitted new standard deal. [CBA Art. II §11, Art. VII §4, Art. XXIX §§2–3](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

An **Exhibit 10** is a standard one-season, non-guaranteed minimum camp contract, with a conversion clause and optional affiliate bonus. A team can have six at a time within the offseason roster maximum. If waived before opening and assigned to the club's G League affiliate under the league's process, the player can earn $5,000 to about $91,000 in 2026–27 by completing 60 consecutive service days under the rule. Instead, if converted before opening day and eligible for a two-way, the conversion protection replaces the affiliate bonus. **Exhibit 9** is the separate camp injury protection form and can coexist before conversion if the signing meets both rules. [CBA Art. II §§3(r)–(s), 11(h), Art. VII §4(k), Exhibit A Exhibits 9–10](https://nbpa.com/cba/)

**Maximum salaries and extensions.** Opening max is the greater of 105% of the player's previous salary or the cap share for his NBA service: 25% for 0–6 years ($41,240,250 in 2026–27), 30% for 7–9 ($49,488,300), 35% for 10+ ($57,736,350). The fifth-year performance max can go to 30% with the incumbent for an eligible fourth-year player, often negotiated as a rookie-scale extension with an award escalator. A deal using that higher share must cover at least four new seasons before any option year. The qualifying results are All-NBA or Defensive Player of the Year in the last season or twice in the last three, or Most Valuable Player once in the past three. The 2023 agreement permits up to five added years on ordinary rookie-scale extensions and removed the former roster limit on designated rookie extensions. The **designated veteran** route, commonly called the **supermax**, can reach 35% early for eligible veterans with eight or nine years at free-agent signing, or seven or eight at qualifying extension signing with one or two years left. Same award standard and service with the original team, or only changes by trade in the first four cap years under contract. A designated veteran free-agent contract runs five years. The designated veteran extension covers six seasons total from signing, including the remaining original years. The 2023 agreement dropped the old two-player limit. The signing carries a one-year trade bar. Certain deals covering four or more seasons and extending past age 38 also move later salary charges forward under the over-38 rule. A rookie-scale extension runs from July 6 to the October deadline before year four and starts after that year. An ordinary veteran extension has different timing, term and pay rules. For example, ordinary starting extension pay can reach 140% of the greater of relevant prior pay or estimated average salary, subject to the player's max and applicable term rules. [CBA Art.

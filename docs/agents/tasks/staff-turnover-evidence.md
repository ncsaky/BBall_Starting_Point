# NBA staff turnover evidence

## TL;DR

- NBA teams averaged about **two head-coach firings during the season and three after it** in 2009–10 through 2025–26.
- **Wins versus preseason expectations** helped predict summer firings a little. Tenure, playoff results and a new front office gave mixed results.
- In the comparable holdout seasons, teams changed about **one listed assistant under a returning coach and five to six under a different coach**.
- **Keep a neutral coordinator eligible for renewal.** An overall grade around 60 is a better starting point for a stable staff than the current 68.
- The executive ledger found **21 clear firings**. Trainer records show continuity in a smaller sample. Scouting and player development mostly have individual examples.

## 1. Head coaches

Each year is the end of an NBA season. The offseason starts after that team's final regular-season game and runs through the next opening night. Playoff teams’ departures after their final playoff game fall in the offseason column. A permanent coach who is publicly dismissed counts as a firing. Contract nonrenewals, mutual splits, retirements, medical leave and promotion to the front office have their own labels. The firing columns exclude two explicitly dismissed interims, Kim Hughes in 2010 and Alvin Gentry in 2022. A one-night stand-in and a stretch where the regular coach still received official credit, such as Luke Walton covering for Steve Kerr in 2015–16, fall outside the ledger. Head-coach firings averaged **2.1 in season and 2.8 afterward** in 2010–2022, then **1.8 and 2.8** in 2023–2026. Including nonrenewals of permanent coaches adds **12** summer exits in the fit and **one** in the holdout. All exits and interim handoffs together averaged **9.8 changes per year** in the fit and **8.3** in the holdout. 2016–17 was the one year with zero changes. An interim counted as kept if they opened the following season as head coach of the same team. That happened **10 of 33** times in the fit and **7 of 12** in the holdout. Some already had their interim tag removed during the season, including Boston's Joe Mazzulla. Portland's Tiago Splitter moved to Chicago after his Portland stint, which counts as an interim exit and a head-coach hire elsewhere. Treat the 30% fit and 58% holdout as a range with small samples. [NBA on the 2016–17 league-wide continuity](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-coaches-no-firings-continuity-longer-contracts) · [Boston promoted Mazzulla](https://www.nba.com/news/celtics-name-joe-mazzulla-coach-gets-extension) · [Chicago hired Splitter](https://www.nba.com/news/bulls-hire-tiago-splitter-as-coach)

A finished permanent coaching spell lasted a median of **230 regular-season games** in the fit and **273** in the holdout, or about **2.8 and 3.3 full seasons**. This is the median among completed spells. Active long stays such as Erik Spoelstra's do not enter the median. Counting games lets an interim-to-permanent coach keep the games already coached. Gregg Popovich's long San Antonio spell ended after a medical absence, and Mitch Johnson kept the resulting job. ### What predicts a firing

The forecast is the opening win total published by **SportsOddsHistory.com** through Covers. The gap is team win percentage minus the projected percentage, shown as wins per 82 games. Use the scheduled 66 games in 2011–12 and 72 games in 2020–21. The shortened 2019–20 season stays in the turnover counts and sits out the forecast fit. The summer model uses team results and the tenure of the permanent coach at the end of the regular season. Interim-only staffs get an interim decision. The training data supply each probability, with 12 extra cases at the league-wide rate to steady small groups. [SportsOddsHistory.com 2010s win totals](https://www.covers.com/sportsoddshistory/nba-regular-season-win-total-results-by-team-2010s/) · [SportsOddsHistory.com 2020s win totals](https://www.covers.com/sportsoddshistory/nba-regular-season-win-total-results-by-team/)

| Wins versus forecast, per 82 | Strict firings in fit | Fitted summer chance | Strict firings in holdout |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Below −8 | 8 / 52 | 14% | 2 / 20 |
| −8 to below −3 | 9 / 63 | 14% | 3 / 20 |
| −3 through +3 | 12 / 113 | 11% | 4 / 31 |
| Above +3 | 4 / 109 | 4% | 2 / 41 |

The Brier score is the average squared difference between the predicted chance and what happened. Smaller is better. Each column uses the same 112 held-out summer cases. The strict model had 11 events. Including nonrenewals raised that to 12.

- **Tenure gives context.** Among teams more than eight wins below forecast, the older middle-tenure group (1½ to under 3½ full seasons) had 5 firings in 18 cases. The shortest-tenure group had 3 in 25 and the longest had 0 in 9. The extra accuracy over the forecast-only rule was small. A fixed tenure cliff would depend heavily on a few cases.
- **Playoff rounds shifted across periods.** In the fit, 24 of 152 teams that missed the playoffs fired their coach, versus 3 of 95 teams that won a series. The holdout produced 3 of 51 and 4 of 31. In the fit, Denver fired George Karl after 57 wins and a first-round loss in 2013. Toronto fired Dwane Casey after 59 wins and a second-round sweep in 2018. In the holdout, New York fired Tom Thibodeau after a 51-win season and a conference finals appearance. [NBA on Karl and coaching turnover](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-coaches-no-firings-continuity-longer-contracts) · [NBA on Casey's exit](https://www.nba.com/cavaliers/news/features/movement-180919) · [AP on Thibodeau's exit](https://apnews.com/article/7f0b4335a3833f9be697cec148993639)
- **In-season pressure has a different scale.** A preseason forecast of 7% per permanent opening coach predicted 8.3 holdout firings, and seven occurred. Splitting by opening tenure made its error worse (.05758 versus .05548). At dismissal, the typical fired opener was running about **7 wins below preseason pace** in the fit and **6 below** in the holdout. Those figures describe the fired coaches at their exit dates. Measuring the chance of being fired at a given checkpoint would require the records of all coaches still at risk on that same date. Adrian Griffin's 30–13 Milwaukee record was running ahead of forecast when he was dismissed. [NBA on Griffin's dismissal and replacement](https://www.nba.com/news/bucks-introduce-doc-rivers)
- **Front-office handoffs can matter on their own timetable.** Detroit hired Trajan Langdon before dismissing Monty Williams after 14 wins in 2024. Denver announced Michael Malone's dismissal and Calvin Booth's nonrenewal together in April 2025. Dallas hired Masai Ujiri in May 2026 and announced a mutual split with Jason Kidd two weeks later. For a time-ordered model, the front-office flag used changes during the preceding offseason or by the end of the current regular season. Six of 53 such teams fired a coach in the older sample. In the holdout, zero of 12 did. Same-summer decisions need reliable announcement order before they can serve as model inputs. [Detroit report](https://apnews.com/article/9a458faa93eac2bb380f8fe910fe7ff6) · [Denver announcement](https://www.nba.com/nuggets/news/denver-nuggets-relieve-head-coach-michael-malone-of-duties-will-not-extend-calvin-booth) · [Dallas announcement](https://www.nba.com/news/mavs-part-ways-jason-kidd)

### Where fired coaches go

A different NBA head job by the next opening night was confirmed for **9 of 64** strict firings in the fit and **5 of 18** in the holdout. For the 18 holdout firings, the first documented destinations or next staff listings also include two assistants, two broadcasters, one consultant and one high-school coach. Seven have no role verified by that cutoff. New head jobs can arrive after a gap, as Taylor Jenkins's Milwaukee hire in 2026 shows. ## 2. Lead assistants and coordinators

The comparison uses named assistant and associate head coaches in opening-season media guides. It joins the old and new staff by person. A departure means a name stops appearing in the assistant list. A promotion, a job elsewhere, an exit from coaching or a change in how the guide lists a role can all produce that result. The head coach is compared with the prior season's opener, so the first summer after an in-season hire still counts as the new coach's first summer. The wide sample starts in summer 2020. Earlier years have club examples. The 2026–27 assistant list comes from the coaches' association in November, a later snapshot with a broader list of assistants. ## 4. Front-office heads

Count the highest basketball decision-maker, whether titled general manager, president or executive vice president. The firing counts are **documented minimums**. The 81 recorded leadership changes include resignations, ownership decisions, reassignments and replacements. One club counts once when it removes two executives at once, as Chicago did with Artūras Karnišovas and Marc Eversley in 2026. Phoenix moving James Jones to adviser and Denver's decision against extending Booth appear as reassignments and nonrenewals, respectively. Strict firings averaged **1.1 per season** in the fit and **1.8** in the holdout. The fit adds six announced nonrenewals and the holdout adds one. Mutual splits and title changes have their own entries, including cases a report might colloquially call a firing. The playoff-drought rule used a fitted 4.4% chance versus 0.5% for other teams and slightly beat the flat-rate Brier score in the holdout (.03989 versus .04048). It covered just five holdout firings and missed the increase in frequency. The two added data filters require a previous season in the study and usable preseason expectations. Raw record helped a little, while wins relative to forecast did worse than the flat rate. These rates describe documented firings, with unknowns in the comparison group. They are directional evidence for an owner's review after repeated missed playoffs. The published NBA and NFL study also associates exits with winning, owners, previous turnover and paired coach and executive changes. [Published study on general manager and head coach exits](https://www.emerald.com/sbm/article/14/1/1/1221463/General-manager-and-head-coach-exits-in-the-NBA)

Washington fired Tommy Sheppard after two 35–47 seasons and explicitly cited missing the playoffs. Orlando dismissed Rob Hennigan after five consecutive misses. Stronger results can coexist with a departure. Portland fired Neil Olshey after a conduct investigation and eight consecutive playoff seasons. Dallas fired Nico Harrison after the Luka Dončić trade and a slow start. Boardroom priorities, investigations, ownership changes and roster transactions are visible in some releases. Consistent historical labels and comparable exposures are scarce. ## 5. Rules to try in the game

| Decision | Suggested starting rule |
|---|---|
| Summer head-coach review | Use the save's preseason team forecast and scale the actual minus forecast gap to 82 games. Start with roughly **14%** below −3 wins, **11%** within three and **4%** above +3. The below −8 split produced nearly the same fitted rate. Use tenure as a small modifier if needed. Let owners occasionally make a change even after a good season. |
| In-season head-coach review | Aim for roughly **two league-wide firings per season**, or a **6–7% season-level rate** among opening incumbents. Concentrate reviews on sustained misses versus the forecast. The evidence gives a season-level frequency and the record at exit. It leaves the chance per review date open. |
| Interim review | Start around **one in three** kept through the following opening. Let the odds vary with the situation and market. 2023–2026 threw up a higher, noisier rate. Interim changes create natural promotion openings for existing assistants. |
| Assistant movement | With the same head coach, target roughly **75–85% annual retention** per slot. When a different head coach takes over, target **one or two changes**, with two more likely. Sampling two names from each real bench gives a fit of **61% neither, 29% one, 9% both** under a returning coach and **11%, 37%, 52%** under a different coach. The comparable holdout gives **71%, 24%, 5%** and **8%, 27%, 65%**. Those are proxies for the game's two slots, based on all listed assistant roles. |
| Coordinator renewal | Give an incumbent with neutral skills and a stable fit a path to renewal at **about 60 overall**, or judge neutrality directly from the three skill ratings at 60. The actual neutral overall grades range from **59.7 to 66.6** with a mean of **62.7**. Use the incumbent's fit, role-specific evidence and genuine upgrades to decide. A staff change drawn earlier in the year should count toward the same annual turnover target. Expiry-only renewal odds and an exact grade cut are gameplay choices. |
| Other staff and executives | Review role-specific performance and allow movement between NBA and G League jobs. Keep a separate, slower support-staff review cycle. For a league-wide executive change, use about **one to two confirmed firing events per year** as a starting scale, with additional reassignments and voluntary moves. Weight repeated playoff misses alongside ownership and roster direction. |

The current stable-fit expiry branch checks grade 68, requires the team to meet expectations, and excludes an unresolved failure trigger. The stronger grade branch starts at 76 for head coaches and coordinators. Moving only the stable-fit bar would help neutral coordinators qualify in ordinary cases. The fit also points toward reviewing the separate veto for an underperforming team, where the current coordinator rule uses grade 88. Team wins alone have thin evidence for blaming a specific assistant.

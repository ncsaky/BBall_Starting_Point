# Player input data audit

## TL;DR

- Audited the evidence feeding all 94 player inputs, including 13 Basketball Index seasons. The findings are for source fixes and leave the player model intact.
- The two drive foul rates give almost the same evidence two votes. The shooting-fouls-drawn column gives contact-seeking preferences a weighted zero for every player and season.
- The latest isolation foul-drawn column is all zero. In that same season, every positive isolation turnover rate exceeds 100%, although dividing by 100 preserves the rankings used today.
- Two purported paint-shooting columns exactly match pull-up columns in all 13 seasons. Movement Distance Rating is zero for 98% of 2025–26 players with regular minutes.
- Fix the capture fields and the way repeatability handles ties first. Then check the remaining weak proxies against real events before changing evidence weights.

## How much the suspect columns change the evidence

I reran the Basketball Index scoring step with each suspect column removed in memory. Removing the all-zero shooting-fouls column changed the contact-seeking evidence score for all 567 joined players with scores. The median absolute change was 6.48 points on the evidence scale and the largest was 11.23. Removing both copied paint columns moved that score by a median 3.34 points and up to 19.04. Keeping one drive-foul column moved the foul-drawing score by a median 1.77 points and up to 9.61. Removing Movement Distance Rating left 567 players without a Basketball Index score for the offensive side of effort allocation. Dropping the already zero-weight isolation foul column or dividing the isolation turnover values by 100 changed zero scores. These comparisons stop before the builder blends in the existing estimates and analyst judgments.

## Repeatability, scale and direction

- The repeatability screen uses a minimum of 1,000 minutes per player in both seasons and 40 matched players. It gives identical values the same rank. Below 0.30 is a weak signal. Among the actively routed Basketball Index columns, 24 fell below that level in at least one of the two build-window pairs. The strongest warnings include contact finishes (0.068), roll-man points per possession (0.130), stable corner three-point percentage (0.138), and off-ball screen points per possession (0.141). Those figures are the weaker of each column's two recent comparisons. Use actual attempts or tagged plays where possible. A change in role or skill can also lower repeatability.
- The 2024–25 **Stable Step Out Of Bounds Turnovers Per 75** check pointed the wrong way for handle security after dividing by scoring opportunities. The oriented correlation was about −0.23 on 260 players. The check compares one turnover subtype with all box-score turnovers per finished play. Review the subtype on comparable possessions before changing its negative sign. The mapping gives it a 0.25 base vote and a 0.224 vote after repeatability. The same flag appears in 2019–20 (−0.23, 142 players). **Floater Talent** had a negative correlation with overall field-goal percentage in 2015–16 and 2018–19. Overall field-goal percentage does not isolate floaters, so those comparisons do not establish a reversed sign.
- The positive checks include both provider snapshots' three-point and free-throw conversion. The April snapshot matches the public box-score direction and fraction scale, with rank correlations of 0.986 for three-point accuracy and 0.992 for free throws. Its three-point attempt share and free-throw rate use percentage points. Its assists and three-point attempts per 75 are about 36 times the corresponding per-minute rates, consistent with roughly two possessions per minute. The apparent difference between stabilized rim defense and the raw rim-defense delta is compatible with a fraction versus percentage points. The builder ranks those columns individually.
- The Basketball Index capture also contains historical runs of constant zeros in three-point contests, block rate on contests, deflections, loose-ball recoveries and screen assists. The affected seasons predate the current build window. Separate those years from genuine zero events if using the historical series for new work.
- Copy checks compare every registered numeric measure with the other numeric columns in its capture or source, using Pearson correlation above 0.98 on shared player rows. Known aliases chosen one at a time and correlated makes/attempts are present in the machine-readable results. They do not create two evidence votes.

## Coverage and gaps

- The audit scanned eight observed Basketball Index Values batches from 2013–14 through 2025–26. It checked 194 mapped or directly read columns against up to 662 captured fields, including exposure and sample-volume dependencies, plus the two measured tendency formulas. The mapping lists 187 distinct metric labels across 66 inputs. Three unmatched alternate labels are only on the older percentile route, which these captures do not qualify for.
- The other local evidence comprises both Crafted snapshots, ESPN career totals, Basketball-Reference dunk and close-shot shares, the 2025–26 calibration player table, combine testing, StatMuse shot bins, roster measurements and roles, the prior trait snapshot, expert packets, and attributed analyst claims. The two Crafted pulls are March and April of the same season. Combine cohorts, analyst claims, expert packets and roster fields have no season-to-season player panel. Several are used for position, role or uncertainty context. The StatMuse location evidence comes from 32 selected players and all 32 reconcile to their shot-bin totals.
- No independent event sample here isolates shot difficulty, typed passing reads, choice to seek contact, off-ball or transition effort, or a player's available choices under different coach orders. This limits sign checks to measures with defensible outcome comparisons. Some outcome sources count the same real plays as Basketball Index. A high agreement across the two is a unit/sign check with shared outcomes.
- All 573 available audited column-seasons in the recent builder cache agree with the normalized source shards. The retained raw-value text also agrees with the normalized capture for 1,299,564 audited cells. The shards contain 152 differing overlaps among audited cells. The audit keeps the last finite value in batch order, matching the cache. The retained values alone cannot distinguish an export binding error, a scale conversion error, and a rounding choice. The report distinguishes observed anomalies from suspected causes.
- Player counts are per capture season or matched cohort. Joined counts show potential reach of the Basketball Index vote, before any player-specific sample modifiers or later analyst changes. March Crafted flags count rows in that snapshot. Aliases, later sources and fallbacks can reduce actual use. The JSON gives each linked input, its weight or formula, and the available player and season counts.

# Stage 1 E: old-scale rating threshold inventory

**Result for review:** The largest direct risks are free-agent bidding, free-agent retention and pricing, rotation minutes, trade-want matching, and fantasy draft growth. The default old-scale data have no overall rating at or above 82; converting that into an arbitrary “superstar” tier would create behavior that currently does not occur through that rating condition. Many other trade and contract formulas use a separate asset-value or player-ability scale and are not mechanically convertible from these summaries.

**Scope and method.** Read-only code audit of direct calls to `player_attribute_summary` and `player_display_attribute_summary` in `src/nba_gm_data`, with their reachable rating-consuming helpers, generated trade-want conditions, and associated GUI consumers. This is an inventory of code paths, not a simulated comparison and not an implementation. `scripts/` and tests are diagnostic consumers, not game AI; they are noted below. `player_attribute_summary` returns one-decimal values; `player_display_attribute_summary` clamps and rounds those to the same old scale. The report calculates every reported individual-player percentile by loading `data/canonical/universe_2026_27_offseason.json` directly and calling `save.player_display_attribute_summary` for all **691** players; the rotation subset is the **365** whose raw `minutes_projection >= 15`. This uses the canonical metadata and default model settings, no save overlays, injury adjustment, hypothetical rotations, or minutes normalization. Reproduce with `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python output/s1e/build_inventory.py`; scratch files are not product deliverables.

**Reading percentiles.** Each pair is the percentage of the stated population with the named old display field *strictly below* the old constant; e.g. `60: 77.42%/57.53%` means below 60 in all/rotation players. Values at exactly the cutoff are excluded; preserve strict versus inclusive comparison and ties in implementation. `Q(field, p)` means the new-scale field value at percentile p. `P(field)` below means the current league empirical fraction strictly below a player's new-scale field; the numeric thresholds in the suggested expressions are illustrative rounded all-player fractions from this snapshot, not a recommendation to round in code. On the same snapshot `P(x) >= P_old(c)` reproduces `x >= c` for observed ordered values with this convention, subject to ties and changes in player ordering; new input-derived ratings will not generally preserve old ordering. A value of 100% for a cutoff above every observed value means **none** pass; `P >= 1` is not a robust permanent star rule for a changing league. Choose explicitly whether that empty historical condition should stay empty. For team averages, gaps, mixed scores, headroom and caps, an individual-player percentile does not describe the actual compared quantity; these are explicitly labeled and require a distribution of that quantity or a converted scale, not a made-up percentile.

**Proposed mapping:** freeze precise old breakpoints as baseline population fractions; resolve the corresponding new-scale league quantile by field, preserve ties and operators, and recalculate slopes, growth offsets and mixed-unit weights separately. Decide whether the league population is all active players or a stable reference cohort; the all/rotation figures below describe materially different decisions. Do not translate the 15 hidden simulation traits or `current_ability`/asset valuation by assuming they are these summaries.

## Ten thresholds or hinges most likely to change AI behavior if converted badly

1. **Free-agent offer count:** overall 60 and 65 (`play.py:5025–5027`) determines how many teams bid.
2. **Released free-agent fallback pay:** overall 55 (`save.py:8961`) changes annual salary, claim cost and release plans.
3. **Roster retention upgrade:** mixed retention score 8 and cost-adjusted gate (`save.py:9032–9037`) controls whether teams cut for a signing.
4. **Free-agent pool retention:** overall 58, 53 and 50 (`save.py:8432–8434`) determines who stays eligible.
5. **Trade-want overall minimums:** 50–56 (`trade_wants.py:291–320`) change which players the search sees as matches.
6. **Trade-want need minimums:** defense 78 versus other specific skills 58 (`trade_wants.py:295–302`) can disproportionately change target types.
7. **Center spacing:** 65 (`trade_roster.py:94,200`) changes trade forecast minutes and overlap penalties.
8. **Rotation floors and caps:** overall 61, 68 and 75 (`save.py:6676,6694–6697`) alter playing time and trade forecasts.
9. **Fantasy draft headroom and 99 ceiling:** `fantasy_draft.py:241–265,458` alter future value and ordering, especially young and elite players.
10. **Free-agent fit:** shooting, defense and creation at 55, plus the 2.5-point position gap (`play.py:5080,5099–5101`) changes offer interest and projected opportunity.

Review these with market activity, contract distributions, rotation behavior, and both project scorecards after implementation. This document does not establish that the proposed percentile substitutions preserve outcomes on the new model.
